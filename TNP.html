02 · INTRODUCTION

Nuclear binding energy

Nuclear binding energy is a fundamental property of atomic nuclei that provides important information about nuclear stability and the interaction between protons and neutrons.

Understanding the variation of binding energy with proton number (Z), neutron number (N), and mass number (A) is therefore an important subject in nuclear physics.

Several nuclear models have been developed to explain nuclear binding energy, including the Liquid Drop Model, represented by the semi-empirical mass formula (SEMF), and the Nuclear Shell Model.

03 · NUCLEAR MODELS

Semi-Empirical Mass Formula

The semi-empirical mass formula (SEMF), also known as the Bethe–Weizsäcker formula, is a mathematical expression used in nuclear physics to approximate the mass and total binding energy of an atomic nucleus.

04 · MODIFIED NUCLEAR MODEL

Inner core and outer surface

The model separates a nucleus into two regions to investigate the structural distinction between the inner core and the outer surface region.

Inner core region

The inner core is composed of symmetric proton–neutron pairs: Z protons and Z neutrons, so Ncore = Z. The core contains 2Z nucleons.

Outer surface region

When N > Z, the remaining N − Z excess neutrons form a surface (skin) region. The mass number is A = 2Z + (N − Z) = Z + N.

V0

Core–surface interaction potential

Unpaired surface neutrons are assumed to move in a core–surface interaction potential generated by the inner core.

05 · MODEL EQUATIONS

Modified core–surface model

The presentation relates the measured binding energy to the core terms and the contribution of the excess surface neutrons.

Core–surface potential
V0 = [−BE(A, Z) + 2avZ − εp·2Z(2Z − 1) − acZ2/(2Z)1/3] / (A − 2Z) MeV
Modified binding-energy expression
BE(A, Z) = avA − εp·2Z(2Z − 1) − acZ2/A1/3 − V0(A − 2Z)
Energy contributions
BE = BEv + BEp + BEc + EH
V0(A − 2Z) = −BE(A, Z) + avA − εp·2Z(2Z − 1) − acZ2/A1/3
A

Volume energy

Represents binding due to the strong nuclear force acting uniformly throughout the nucleus. Binding energy per nucleon is roughly constant, so total binding energy is proportional to the number of nucleons and thus to A. This is the dominant, attractive term. Because the strong nuclear force is short-range, each nucleon binds only to its neighbouring nucleons.

BEv ∝ A
BEv = avA
BEv = 2avZ (symmetric core)
B

Pairing term in the core

The inner core contains Z protons and Z neutrons, for a total of 2Z nucleons. The presentation gives the number of core nucleon pairs as Npair = 2Z(2Z − 1). If εp is the interaction energy associated with each pair, the core pairing energy is:

BEp = εp · 2Z(2Z − 1)
C

Coulomb energy in the core

Coulomb energy comes from electrostatic repulsion between protons. It is inversely proportional to the nuclear radius and increases the energy, making the nucleus less stable as proton number increases. The presentation uses Z − 1 ≈ Z for Z > 1.

BEc = acZ(Z − 1)/A1/3
BEc ≈ acZ2/(2Z)1/3 (core approximation)
D

Core–surface potential term

This is the asymmetry-dependent component of the average nuclear potential, proportional to neutron excess, A − 2Z = N − Z. In the model, it arises from the interaction between the core and the excess neutrons in the surface region.

EH = −V0(A − 2Z)

06 · RESULTS & DISCUSSION

Potential across isotopes and isotones

ISOTOPES · Z FIXED

For isotopes, proton number Z remains constant while neutron number increases. The presentation reports that V0 decreases as additional neutrons occupy the surface region and increase neutron excess N − Z. More surface neutrons share the core interaction potential, reducing the average core–surface interaction strength per neutron.

ISOTONES · N FIXED

For isotones, neutron number N remains constant while proton number increases. The presentation reports that V0 increases as more proton–neutron pairs are added to the core. The larger core provides stronger attractive interaction with the fixed number of surface neutrons.

07 · CONCLUSION

Study conclusions

  • The calculated core–surface interaction potential V0 shows a clear dependence on neutron number and proton number for different nuclear systems.
  • For isotopes, V0 decreases with increasing neutron number because additional surface neutrons increase neutron excess (N − Z) and reduce the average core–surface interaction strength.
  • For isotones, V0 increases with increasing proton number because additional protons enhance the proton–neutron core structure and strengthen its interaction with surface neutrons.
  • The decrease of V0 is more significant in light nuclei and becomes gradual in medium and heavy nuclei due to the formation of a stable neutron-rich surface region.
  • Overall, the results support the modified nuclear model: the potential varies with neutron excess in isotopes and with core-size variation in isotones.
