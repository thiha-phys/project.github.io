GROUP 2 MATERIALS PROJECT

Precipitation Synthesis and Phase Analysis of Cu/Mg Single- and Co-Doped β-Tricalcium Phosphate Bioceramics via X-Ray Diffraction

A study of co-precipitated β-TCP powders, ionic substitution, phase stability, lattice parameters, crystallite size, and crystallinity using X-ray diffraction.

Explore the project ↓

01 · AIM

Research aim

To synthesize undoped, single-doped, and co-doped (Cu/Mg) β-tricalcium phosphate (β-TCP) bioceramics by the co-precipitation method, and to evaluate the impact of ionic substitution on their crystallographic structure, phase stability, and lattice parameters using X-ray Diffraction (XRD).

02 · INTRODUCTION

Bone repair and bioceramics

The bone-defect problem

Bone defects can result from trauma, tumor removal, infection, and aging-related bone loss. Bone has natural healing properties, but large bone defects may require surgery.

Current surgical treatments include autografts, allografts, xenografts, and synthetic bone grafts. Autografts are considered the gold standard but have limited availability. Allografts carry a risk of immune response. Xenografts raise complex religious and ethical considerations. Synthetic bone grafts are a promising alternative.

β

β-Tricalcium phosphate is chemically similar to bone mineral and supports bone replacement.

β-TRICALCIUM PHOSPHATE · β-TCP

A synthetic bone-graft material

β-TCP has chemical similarity to bone mineral, excellent osteoconductivity, and a degradation rate that closely matches natural bone remodeling.

Limitations that motivate modification
  • Brittle
  • No antibacterial property
  • Limited bioactivity

Modification is needed to improve functionality.

06 · RESULTS & DISCUSSION

Structure and phase response

✓

All synthesized samples matched β-TCP PDF card no. 1517238, with no detected secondary phases: CuO, MgO, hydroxyapatite (HAp), or α-TCP.

β-0.15Cu · SINGLE Cu2+ DOPING

Lattice response

  • The observed (0210) dominant peak shifts toward higher 2θ relative to β-TCP.
  • The a-axis expands slightly: 10.4418 → 10.4438 Å.
  • The c-axis contracts slightly: 37.4436 → 37.4382 Å.
  • Unit-cell volume expands: 3535.55 → 3536.41 Å3.
  • The presentation attributes the change to the smaller Cu2+ ionic radius (0.73 Å) occupying Ca2+ sites (1.00 Å).
β-1Mg · β-0.15Cu1Mg · Mg & CO-DOPING

Lattice contraction

  • A noticeable shift to higher 2θ is reported, more prominent in co-doped β-0.15Cu1Mg.
  • Both a and c axes contract sharply, with unit-cell volume reported at 3514.07 Å3.
  • The discussion cites ionic radii Mg2+ = 0.72 Å and Cu2+ = 0.73 Å.
  • The interpretation follows Bragg’s law, nλ = 2d sin θ.
95.23%–96.36%High long-range crystallinity maintained across samples
~95.23%Reported for single Cu and Mg doping; attributed to point defects and microstrain
95.35%Crystallinity reported for co-doped β-0.15Cu1Mg
86.05–99.39 nmAverage crystallite-size range reported across doping
92.88 nmCrystallite size reported for Cu/Mg co-doped sample

The presentation concludes that doping levels at or below 1.0% allow grain growth while maintaining structural phase purity. It also reports that Cu/Mg co-doping restores structural order and promotes crystallite growth.

07 · CONCLUSION

Project conclusions

01

Successful synthesis

Undoped, Cu/Mg single- and co-doped β-TCP powders were successfully synthesized by the co-precipitation method.

02

Phase purity

XRD analysis confirmed single-phase β-TCP structures across all samples, without secondary oxide phases.

03

Lattice response

Cu/Mg co-doping produced a prominent unit-cell volume shrinkage, attributed to Mg2+ and Cu2+ ions with smaller ionic radii replacing Ca2+ sites.

04

Crystallinity and growth

Cu/Mg co-doped β-TCP retained high crystallinity (95.35%) and stable crystallite growth (92.88 nm), making it a potential candidate for further biomedical evaluation.
